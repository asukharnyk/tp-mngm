Reading it — how the columns, bars and colours work
The first two columns stay put while you scroll the timeline sideways. Purple rows are Design System features, teal are features owned by other teams, collapsed by default with the same fields. Bars sit in the week they fall in; the amber column is the current week. Ticket bars sit inside their epic's window, packed back from the due date; done and design-review tickets get no bar. Red bars mark epics whose start has already passed — the due date is out of reach at that estimate. Epics are ordered by nearest due date. keep in DS marks an other-team epic that should stay with the design system because the platform consumes it; hand over marks one with a single consumer that belongs with the owning team. Hover either for the reason, and use Ownership to show only one set. The reasoning is in the Summary tab. Blocker chips are links — each shows its own due date, cleared ones are struck through. Backward start is calculated backwards from the Jira due date, not planned by anyone: an epic needing 6 days and due 16 Sep must start 9 Sep (weekends skipped). The Sprint column reads "4 in S37" — four of that row's tickets sit in the active sprint. A red backward start means that date is already behind us — the work had to begin earlier than today to hit the due date.
The calendar draws two different things. Backward bars are computed — due date minus the estimate, weekends skipped — so they show when work would have to run. Sprint fill shades the whole cell for weeks a ticket is assigned to a sprint in Jira — amber for the active sprint, pale blue for any other, near-white once the ticket is finished. Where the fill sits later than the bar, the plan of record is already behind the arithmetic. Each layer toggles on its own.
Scenario changes what the solid bars mean. Due dates schedules each epic backwards from its own Jira date, so bars can overlap and sit in the past. The four team sizes instead queue every epic forward from tomorrow, one after another in this same order, at that headcount — so the bars stop overlapping and the readout on the right gives the date the whole scope lands. Red still marks an epic finishing after its Jira due date. Sprint fill never changes: it is always what Jira says.
Ordering. Epics carrying work in the active sprint are lifted to the top, and so are the features holding them; everything else keeps due-date order. Epics only — the default — drops the feature rows entirely and lists the epics flat; click one to open its tickets. Collapse all brings the feature rows back.
Walk me steps through the six things worth noticing, arrow-pointing at each one with the current numbers in it. Arrow keys move between steps, Esc closes.
A manual drag order, once set for a feature, overrides this. The sprint header shows points · tickets closed in grey for each finished sprint, and the planned commitment in blue for the active and upcoming ones — planned is what sits in the sprint, not what has landed.
Drag the ⠿ handle on an epic row to move it up or down inside its feature. That order is what the team scenarios queue against, so it changes which epics land early or late — and therefore which turn red — but not the final date: the total only moves with headcount or scope. The order is kept in this browser and survives a reload; Reset order puts it back to due-date order.
Min / Mid / Max redraw everything. Untick a column to drop it; Timeline off makes the table fill the width.
Every epic but one is due by end of September — all on sprint boundaries. Set against the hours in Scenario 1, those dates cannot all hold; red due dates are the ones already out of reach.
Design blockers are cleared. The XXD tickets blocking these epics are Done (struck through). Live blockers are engineering-internal.
